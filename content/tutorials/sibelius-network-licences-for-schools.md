Imagine a class of students ready to compose, only to discover that several workstations cannot start Sibelius. It's a stressful moment for any teacher — and a useful reminder that **music software licensing is part of classroom planning**, not just purchasing.

Sibelius licensing and administration have evolved across product generations. Before following older network-server instructions, check **the exact Sibelius edition, licence type and current Avid documentation**. Features available to a legacy network site licence may not apply to current account- or team-based arrangements.

## 1. Establish which licence model you own

Start with an inventory of purchased entitlements, version numbers, device types and the number of pupils expected to work simultaneously. A traditional floating network licence may allocate seats through a local licensing service; another licence model may use different activation and administration procedures.

Do not assume that installing Sibelius on 30 computers automatically provides 30 simultaneous seats. Check the agreement and available seats before rollout.

## 2. Plan the classroom deployment

Work with your school's IT administrator to record:

- Supported operating systems and software version.
- Entitlement or activation method for your specific licence.
- Required permissions and network access according to official vendor instructions.
- A test workstation on each classroom network or subnet.
- A documented process for replacing or rebuilding computers.

For older licence-server deployments, confirm the correct server address and firewall settings from documentation for that *specific release*. Avoid opening ports based solely on an older online tutorial.

## 3. What about students working at home?

Remote access and temporary offline use depend on the licence plan and software generation. Some legacy deployments support borrowing or checking out seats, but that is **not a universal Sibelius feature**.

Before promising home access, verify eligibility, check-out limitations, expiry rules and the school's device policy with the provider.

## 4. Troubleshoot connection or activation errors systematically

If a pupil cannot open Sibelius, work from the simplest checks upwards.

1. **Scope the problem.** Does it affect one machine or the entire class?
2. **Confirm the installation.** Compare its edition and version with a working computer.
3. **Check connectivity.** Can the affected machine reach the approved licensing endpoint or configured legacy server?
4. **Review entitlements.** Are seats available and are the relevant accounts or devices authorised?
5. **Record the error.** Capture the exact message and any diagnostic details before making changes.

A successful ping establishes only basic reachability; it does not prove that licensing traffic or authentication is working. Avoid reinstalling every workstation until you know the cause.

## 5. Give teachers a reliable fallback

Keep a locally available worksheet, listening activity or printed notation exercise ready for the rare lesson when software access fails. Also retain a concise setup record so a new IT colleague can troubleshoot without starting from zero.

## Before you purchase or renew

Ask the vendor which licence models are available **today**, how seats are allocated, how remote learners are covered and what administration tools your staff will actually receive. This is particularly important if you're upgrading from an older network site licence.
