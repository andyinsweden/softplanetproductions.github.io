If you are new to improvisation, using fewer notes can make music feel more creative, not less. The **pentatonic scale** contains five notes. That gives you a manageable palette for experimenting with rhythm, repetition and expression without constantly thinking about which key to press.

Here is a simple major pentatonic exercise you can play today, even if you do not read sheet music.

## What are the notes of the C major pentatonic scale?

Play these five white-key notes with your right hand:

**C – D – E – G – A**

You may recognise the notes from C major, but without F and B. Try playing them slowly upwards and downwards, then stop thinking of them as a scale. They are ingredients for your own melody, not an exercise you must always play in order.

The C major pentatonic scale is different from a C **minor** pentatonic scale, which we will try later.

## Exercise 1: Make a melody from three notes

Start with just **C, D and E**. Give each note one beat, then rest for a beat. Try these two ideas:

- **Question:** C – D – E – (rest)
- **Answer:** E – D – C – (rest)

Now invent a different answer. Hold one note longer, repeat another, or leave an extra beat of silence. The change in rhythm is often more noticeable than the change in notes.

## Exercise 2: Add G and A

Expand your right-hand choices to **C, D, E, G and A**. Try a four-beat phrase such as:

**C – E – G – A**

Answer with:

**G – E – D – C**

Play slowly, then change one detail. You might start the answer later, repeat G, or use a longer final C. This is the beginning of musical phrasing.

## Exercise 3: Improvise over a chord progression

Give the left hand one lower bass note at the start of each bar:

| Bar | Chord | Left-hand bass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | C major | C |
| 2 | G major | G |
| 3 | A minor | A |
| 4 | F major | F |

Keep using the same right-hand notes **C, D, E, G and A**. Some notes will sound more settled over certain chords; others add colour or a sense of movement.

Listen for those differences rather than assuming every note should be equally strong. If a note sounds tense, you can move to a nearby chord tone or simply leave space.

For the full chord shapes and smoother changes, see [piano chord progressions for beginners](/tutorials/piano-chord-progressions-for-beginners/).

## How is the minor pentatonic scale different?

For a contrasting sound, try **C minor pentatonic**:

**C – E♭ – F – G – B♭**

These notes make a different musical palette from C major pentatonic. Try a slow **C minor** chord or a repeated C bass note with this scale. Compare its sound with your earlier C major exercise. Do not worry about playing both scales fluently in one session: listen first.

## A five-minute pentatonic practice routine

1. **One minute:** Play the five C major pentatonic notes slowly.
2. **One minute:** Invent two-bar questions using only three notes.
3. **Two minutes:** Use all five notes over C–G–Am–F, changing one rhythm each time.
4. **One minute:** Record a short phrase and choose your favourite musical idea.

The goal is not to fill every beat. A pause can make the next note much more expressive.
